    bejuco

    COLECTIVO NULL

    Sofía Lozano
    Nicolás Casas
    Juan David Ramírez

    ​

    Proposal for the Beca Bienal de Arte 2027

    Arquitectura Sonora · Orquesta Filarmónica de Bogotá

    full screen

    Bejuco asks about the memory of Bogotá through its vegetation. We rarely ask who chose the trees, flowers and shrubs of the city’s parks, squares and gardens. The admiration nature inspires makes us think it is there by chance, but every government also governs nature, its distribution and its species. Not all vegetation was planted, but it is possible to question the memory of what was: what does it tell us?

    The work is an interactive sound installation of suspended ceramic flowers. Each flower holds a distance sensor and a small speaker inside: when someone comes near, the flower sounds, and its sound changes with the closeness of the listener. The sounds range from the songs of birds displaced from the parks to the resonances of the objects that inhabit them: a knock on a post, a statue, people playing. A map of Bogotá with LED lights turns on when someone approaches the corresponding flower.

    The proposal continues the research of antejardín (2025) on the origin of the Parque Nacional, and extends it to the squares and parks founded between the 19th and 20th centuries, drawing on La vida privada de los parques y jardines públicos. Bogotá, 1886-1938, by Claudia Cendales Paredes.

    Above, an interactive render of the installation: you can walk among the flowers, crouch or stand on tiptoe, and listen to how they sound. Headphones are recommended.
