    bejuco. memoria viva

    COLECTIVO NULL

    Sofía Lozano
    Nicolás Casas
    Juan David Ramírez

    ​

    Proposal for the Beca Bienal de Arte 2027

    Arquitectura Sonora · Orquesta Filarmónica de Bogotá

    full screen

    Bejuco. Memoria viva asks about the memory of Bogotá through its vegetation. We rarely ask who chose the trees, flowers and shrubs of the city’s squares and parks. The admiration nature inspires makes us think it is there by chance, but every government also governs nature, its distribution and its species. Not all vegetation was planted, but it is possible to question the memory of what was: what does it tell us? The proposal asks this from a specific place, the garden of the Plaza de Bolívar, and from something that garden lost over time: its sound.

    In 1881 it was decided to surround the statue of Simón Bolívar with a garden, in the care of the gardener Casiano Salcedo, which opened on 20 July 1881. It was the first of the city’s colonial squares to become a public garden: a square area in the middle of the plaza, enclosed by a wrought-iron fence, in the English style, with lamps, planters with shrubs, agapanthus, pines, palms and roses, native and foreign species planted after a European model. That garden no longer exists, and today the plaza is an open stone space where pigeons, ceremonies, concerts and demonstrations mingle.

    The work is an interactive sound installation of fifteen suspended ceramic flowers, inspired by the species recorded in the sources on the garden and arranged along its layout. Each flower holds a distance sensor and a small speaker inside, and sounds in two times: the soundscape of the garden between 1881 and 1910 —birdsong, bells and the everyday sounds of the period, reconstructed through archival research— and the imagined soundscape of the pre-Hispanic Muisca territory, drawn from archaeological and ethnohistorical sources and colonial chronicles. As someone comes closer, the sound of the Muisca territory grows and the sound of the garden recedes. A vinyl plan of the garden, with LED lights, turns on as each flower is approached.

    The work will be installed in an architectural space in La Candelaria during the Bienal de Arte 2027, using the acoustics and the movement of people through the place as part of the composition, so that the public may ask who decides the vegetation of the city and what memory it keeps. It continues the research of antejardín (2025) on the origin of the Parque Nacional, and draws on La vida privada de los parques y jardines públicos. Bogotá, 1886-1938, by Claudia Cendales Paredes.
